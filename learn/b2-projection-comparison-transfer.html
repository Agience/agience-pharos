Entroptics / lesson eight · three reads

Projection,
Comparison,
Transfer

Three questions you can ask about two records. In English they sound like paraphrases of each other. In arithmetic they are three different operations, and on the same real pair they return three different numbers.

Getting them confused is not a philosophical error. It is the kind that produces a plausible figure and a wrong decision.

One pair throughout: the EEG's two halves, the record carried through units A3, A4, A6 and B3.

Three questions

readthe question in words
projectionHow much of the left side lies inside what the right side can resolve?
comparisonHow closely do the two move together, row by row?
transferIf the left side sends its beam at the right, how much arrives?

Each is a real thing you might want to know, and each is a different thing. The first is about overlap. The second is about agreement. The third is about delivery, which depends on the first and on something else besides.

Watch what happens to the numbers. Two of them turn out to be exactly the same quantity wearing different names, and the third — the one that sounds most like the others — is off by more than four times.

Projection — what lies inside

Take the directions the right side actually resolves (here, 1 of a possible 2), and split the left side's frame against them. Everything that lands inside is absorbed; everything else is transmitted and carries on.

energyshare
going in1595.321.000000
absorbed735.960.461326
transmitted859.360.538674

The two parts add back to the whole with a relative error of 1.43e-16 — that is the machine's rounding. The projection is orthogonal, and orthogonal projections conserve exactly. Nothing is lost and nothing is invented.

That exactness is load-bearing later: it is what lets a piece of the platform use “is the leftover bigger than float round-off?” as a real test, with no threshold anyone had to choose.

Comparison — how much they agree

A completely different question, and it is not answered by splitting anything. Line the two records up row by row and ask how much they rise and fall together. Unit A6 did this: the answer is a signed number between −1 and +1, tested against a null that re-pairs the rows.

A factor of 4.5 between them, on the same pair, in the same instant. Neither is wrong. Nearly half the left side lives in directions the right side can resolve — and yet the two records only move together about a tenth of the way.

Lying in someone's subspace is not the same as agreeing with them. A choir and a single voice can occupy the same range and sing different notes.

Transfer — what actually arrives

Delivery needs two things to go right, and projection only checks the first.

delivered  =  energy × participation × τ

Participation is the share with somewhere to land — can the receiver resolve these directions at all? τ is the Greek letter tau, said TOW: how much room the receiver has, relative to the sender, capped at 1.

stepvaluewhat it asks
participation0.461326is there anywhere for it to land?
τ0.988359is there room once it gets there?
delivered, as a share0.455956both, together

Here τ is nearly 1, so almost everything that could land does. When the receiver is the smaller instrument τ falls, and the difference is reflected — coupled, but turned back for want of room.

The two gates are independent. A perfect overlap into a tiny receiver delivers little; a poor overlap into a large one delivers little for the opposite reason. Only their product is the answer.

Two of them are the same number

Look again at what projection reported and what transfer's first gate reported:

projection — share absorbed

0.461326228

transfer — participation

0.461326224

Not similar. The same — they differ by 4.08e-09, which is floating-point dust.

So transfer's first gate is projection, and transfer is projection plus one more independent thing that projection knows nothing about. That relationship is worth holding onto:

  • Ask projection when you want to know what fits.
  • Ask transfer when you want to know what gets through.
  • Ask comparison when you want to know whether they agree — a question neither of the others answers, which is why its number was four times off.

Three names, two operations, and one derived quantity. Knowing which you asked for is most of the skill.

The test that sorts them: change the units

Suppose the left side had been recorded in millivolts: every number a thousand times bigger, the same measurement. Which of these reads should change?

None of the shares. A share is a fraction of the record by itself, so a change of units cancels top and bottom. The energies, on the other hand, must change — and by the square of the factor, because energy is built from squares.

as recorded×1000changed?
energy1595.321.59532e+09×1e+06
delivered727.407.27397e+08×1e+06
comparison — strength0.1023090.102309no
transfer — participation0.4613260.461326no
τ0.9883590.988359no

This is a real check you can run on any read, and it is the fastest way to find out what a number is actually made of. If it moves when you change units, it is an amount. If it does not, it is a proportion.

It also settles which axis the split runs along. Participation is every bit as invariant as comparison, so the split is not between the three reads. It is between shares and amounts, and it cuts across them: projection reports an absorbed energy and an absorbed share, transfer reports a delivered energy and a delivered share, and only the units test tells which is which.

Doing real work — silence stays silence

This split is the most-used instrument read in the entire platform. Here it is deciding what a routing component does with an incoming signal:

absorbed, _t, k = r
return absorbed if k else None          # k == 0 is silence: nothing coupled here

And the rule it is keeping, in the code's own words:

“None is silence: nothing resolved (the null, for free); silence stays silence, never a synthesised answer.”

When nothing couples, the component returns nothing. It does not return its best guess, or the closest thing it had, or an empty-but-valid-looking result. The absence of a signal is itself a finding, and the code is built so that finding survives to the caller.

You have now seen this decision three times: a redundancy of exactly zero in A5, a coupling strength of zero in A6, and silence here. Unit B3 makes it four, with an attenuation constant of zero. It is the same design choice each time, made deliberately — but they are not equally strong. A5’s zero is a property of one fixed record. A6’s is a rate: exactly zero in 192 of 200 shuffles — and so is the silence on this page, as the controls slide measures. Knowing which kind of zero you are quoting is the point, and the unit after next sets the difference out in full.

Doing real work — a threshold nobody chose

The second place is grading: an observer has stated something, and the question is whether what it said is carried by the records it was supposed to be reading. Split what was said against what the corpus resolves, and look at the leftover.

tol = eps * max(1.0, energy) * max(said.shape)
ok  = residual <= tol and absorbed_e > tol
“The tolerance is the projector's own arithmetic, not a choice: an orthogonal projection conserves exactly, so anything above float round-off on this frame is un-absorbed signal.”

That is the exact conservation of the split, established when the frame was first projected, being cashed in. Because the split loses nothing, any leftover above the machine's own rounding must be real — things the observer said that its sources do not carry. No threshold was tuned, and none can drift.

The same function is careful about a subtler thing. It only asks for the instrument after the structural checks, deliberately: “asking for the instrument earlier would turn ‘the observer stated nothing’ into ‘this host cannot measure’, which are different statements.”

Three kinds of no, kept apart: nothing was said, nothing coupled, and nothing could be measured.

Three questions, or one question three ways?

Everything so far is one pair giving three numbers — which is what three genuinely different reads would do, and also what three scalings of the same quantity would do. To tell those apart you need pairs where the reads disagree about the order. Here are six, all on records this course already carries:

Read the second row against the first. Shuffling the rows of one side takes comparison from 0.102309 to an exact zero — and leaves projection and transfer completely unmoved, to all six places. Same two records, one operation, and one read collapses while the other two do not notice. Row order is the whole of what comparison asks about, and none of what projection does.

Now the fifth row. Projection 0.723682 and comparison 0.818888 are both high, and transfer is 0.223357 — a third of either. Transfer is projection and a second gate, and here the second gate is what decides.

The third row is the sharpest of all, and it is not a paradox. A half of the EEG against itself compares at 1.000000 — perfect agreement, as it must — and projects at 0.000000. Because principal_directions resolves 0 modes in a two-column record: there is no subspace to lie inside, so nothing can. Perfectly aligned, and nowhere to land.

Two rows of that table are controls — the shuffle and the noise — and both were a single draw. A single draw says what that permutation, or that sample, happened to do. It does not say what the operation does. Each was run 100 times:

control, over 100 drawsreads an exact zero loudest comparison
row order destroyed (permutation)95 of 1000.075487
the side replaced by noise97 of 1000.058099

Neither control is unanimous. The single permutation in the table above read an exact zero, and one draw reading zero looks like a law. It is a rate: 95 of 100 permutations, and 97 of 100 noise draws. On the draws where comparison resolves something, it reaches 0.075487 and 0.058099 respectively — against the recorded pair's 0.102309 in the first row, which is only 1.36× the loudest thing a null produced.

Two things follow, and the second is the useful one. First, comparison is the read with the least headroom on this pair: the record clears both nulls, and it clears them by a margin a null can nearly reach. Projection and transfer are not close — they read zero on noise in 100 and 100 draws out of 100, with nothing above the floor.

Second, a few percent of nulls resolving is not a fault in the controls. It is the cutoff doing what a cutoff does. A threshold that admitted no null would be a threshold set so high that it read zero on real structure too, and unit A7 measures exactly where this one sits. The lesson is that a control has to be run enough times to see the leak, and one draw never will.

What each of the three actually answers

The practical rule is short, and it is the reason getting these confused is expensive. Ask which question you meant before you quote a number — on the pairs above, a row-shuffle that leaves one read exactly where it was takes another to zero, and a pair that overlaps and agrees still delivers a third of what it holds. Each of those is a different sentence about the same two records.

And an amount is not a share. The last row is energy, which scales with the record and belongs to no comparison — it answers “how much”, never “how much of it”.
