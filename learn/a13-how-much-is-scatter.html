Entroptics / lesson A13 · a read that reports its own uncertainty

How Much of This Is Scatter?

Unit A11 gives three tests a reader runs by hand to find out what a number is measuring. This unit is the same question answered by the instrument, on every read, for nothing.

Why it costs nothing. The decay is a sum over the channels' own autocovariances, so the channels are repeats of the same measurement. How far they disagree is the read's uncertainty, and it is already in the record. Nothing is assumed about the noise and nothing is removed.

noise share  —  how much of the decay is the channels disagreeing
tail share  —  how much of it is decay away from zero lag

The condition to read them by is the library's own: when the noise share approaches the tail share, the width away from zero lag is scatter, and the resolution built on it reads a longer correlation than the record supports.

The instrument publishes both shares together as decay_scatter, alongside the channel count they were measured across.

a record whose decay is resolveda record whose decay is scatter
the terrain grid: noise 0.0021, tail 0.9308 noise, 800×4: noise 0.1034, tail 0.1044

One of those two is a measurement of the ground and the other is a measurement of a random number generator. The ratio between the shares says which, without being told anything about either.

The top three are records whose decay the read resolves. The bottom four are records where the noise share has caught up with the tail, and three of those four are noise or the market's monthly changes, which this course has always put with the noise.

The fourth is the market's prices, and that row is this unit's finding. It reads +9.304 on the ordered axis, which is order. Unit B5 gives it 29.75 resolvable spots — the second-highest count in this course, above the EEG's 23.74. And 91% of the decay that count rests on is the channels disagreeing with each other.

Every number in that row is correct arithmetic and repeats on every run. The spot count is real; what it measures is a decay the record cannot support. That is the whole subject of this course arriving on its own second record.

Why: there is not enough record

A11's first test is to halve the record and read again. Do it to the prices, and watch the scatter ratio:

It gets worse. On all 71 rows the ratio is 0.9088; on segments of 17 it reaches 1.3934. A read given less record reports more scatter, which is the behaviour of a read running out of evidence.

So the prices' row on the previous slide is a shortage of record, and the whole record is 71 rows. There is no cut of this file at which the decay resolves — the file is the cut.

Which is worth stating exactly. The prices have order in them: unit A4 finds it, and unit A3 explains it as a price series moving in small steps, so neighbouring rows sit close together. That order is real. The resolution built on the decay is what 71 rows cannot support, and those are two different claims about one record.

  • The uncertainty was already in the record. The channels are repeats of one measurement, so their disagreement is the read's error bar. It costs no extra data and no assumption about what the noise looks like.
  • It sorts the seven records the way the course does, and then adds one. The three with resolved decay are the terrain and the two readings of the EEG; the four where scatter has caught up are two noise draws, the market's changes — and the prices.
  • The prices are the finding. Ordered by A4, second-most-resolved by B5, and 91% scatter here. Three correct reads, and the third says what the second is built on.
  • Cutting the record makes it worse. 0.9088 over 71 rows becomes 1.3934 over 17, which is what a shortage looks like from the inside.

What this read establishes is how much of one number is the record's own scatter. Whether a resolution built on that number should be quoted is a decision about the study, and this measurement is one input to it. Unit A11 is where the same question gets asked of reads that publish no such share.
