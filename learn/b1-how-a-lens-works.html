Unit B1 · the physics between

How a Lens
Works

Track A taught what a screen reads. This is about the thing that does the reading — and about how little the library is willing to take on faith about it.

A lens is a name and two conversions. entry takes a surface onto the shared basis and is required. inverse brings a frame back out, and is needed only to render through that side or to certify it.

Everything else a lens carries is its own physics: where it balances (zero), how it counts energy (energy), and what it considers noise (null). Each defaults to a derived read and each may be declared. The library's own words: “the system owns the law, the library owns the measurement.”

Measured on the record that has run through A3, A6 and B3 — eeg.dat, 800×4, delay-embedded to 796×16 where a beam of several modes is needed.

A lens is certified, and the certificate can fail

Registering a conversion does not declare it good. The library hands you two certificates, and both can come back negative on a conversion you just registered.

certificatethe questionfails when
lineardoes the conversion pass a beam's modes independently? splitting a mode off, converting it, and recombining stops agreeing with converting the beam whole
certifyis inverse(entry(x)) the surface again? something the instrument could see did not survive the round trip

Neither is a tolerance you choose. Both decide with the same instrument every other unit uses — does the leftover resolve any structure above this screen's own noise floor — so a lens is judged by the reader that will be reading through it.

That is the whole design in one line: the residual is put back on a screen and read. If the instrument cannot see what was lost, nothing was lost as far as anything downstream is concerned.

What a lens must preserve, one: linearity

Two departures, both measured in balance. Additivity converts the modes together and compares against converting them one at a time. Homogeneity converts a scaled frame and compares against scaling the conversion.

Squaring departs on both and is caught: additivity 0.6749. The other two rows are the ones worth sitting with.

Affine reads as linear (2.33e-13). Adding a constant is not a linear map — but the side's zero absorbs a constant offset, and the screen never sees it. What is certified is linearity as the reader experiences it.

A matrix that mixes every mode also reads as linear (2.17e-16) — and it should. “Passes modes independently” does not mean “leaves modes alone”; it means the conversion of a sum is the sum of the conversions. This row is the control on that reading.

A lens that reads linear without being tested

Read linearity on a lens that has not been placed and it comes back additivity = nan, modes = 0 — at 2, 4 and 16 columns. The identity lens reads linear, and that verdict is worth nothing.

The additivity check splits the surface into as many modes as the beam carries, and it gets that number from the beam — so a lens that has never been placed has no beam, gets zero, and the check silently does not run. What is left is homogeneity alone.

The library states the condition. modes is a field on the result for this reason, and its docstring says the check “needs 2”. It sits one line above linear: True.

The tell is the uniformity. The same answer at every width, for conversions that should differ, is a property of the harness: a real property varies with what it is measured on. Three widths returning nan three times is that signature.

What a lens must preserve, two: the round trip

certify asks whether inverse(entry(x)) is the surface again. The decision is a conjunction of two derived checks, and the interesting part is why one of them is not enough.

  1. the residual resolves nothing — put the leftover on its own screen and read it: K_signal == 0 means no structure survived that the instrument could see. The table below reports that screen's largest singular value, written σ (sigma), against the noise floor it is judged by — and where a residual is exactly zero there is no screen to read, so the floor is ∞ (infinity);
  2. the round trip beats returning nothing — residual < 1, where 1 is the residual of the trivial inverse that hands back nothing at all.

Row two is built to defeat check 2 alone: every joule comes back — it is renormalised to the original energy — and the residual is 0.7295, comfortably under 1. Only check 1 catches it, because the direction it threw away is structure and shows up at σ 39.7 against a floor of 26.8.

Row three is built to defeat check 1 alone: the surface is discarded and replaced with noise. Its residual is 1.4032 — worse than returning nothing — and that is check 2's entire job.

And the louder the noise, the better it hides

The docstring claims check 1 alone would pass a noise replacement of any amplitude. That is a claim about a population, so one draw cannot settle it. 1000 draws at each of three amplitudes:

At ×1 it is a coin flip — 566 of 1000, 0.566 — because noise at the signal's own scale sometimes throws up a bump the instrument calls a mode. At ×50 the noise slips past check 1 in 954 draws out of 1000, 0.954.

That is the direction nobody expects. Louder damage is easier to miss by the structural check, because loudness is not structure. Check 2 — the dull one, a single comparison against returning nothing — catches it 1000 out of 1000 at every amplitude.

And the rise stops. ×5 reads 0.944 and ×50 reads 0.954 — a difference of 0.010, which is nothing. The lesson is at the signal's own scale the structural check is a coin flip, and above it the check is simply blind; it is not a slope that keeps climbing.

This slide rested on 20 draws until 2026-08-28 and 20 draws told three stories the population does not support. ×1 read 8/20 — 0.400, which nobody would call a coin flip — while the prose beside it called it one. ×50 read 20 of 20 and the prose called it unanimous. And 19/20 against 20/20 looked like a rise continuing to a ceiling. A draw here is milliseconds: 20 was never the affordable choice, it was the unexamined one.

A conjunction is not belt-and-braces here. Each check has a family of failures only the other one sees, and both families are easy to build by accident.

And what it is allowed to drop

Not everything that fails to cross is a fault. Transfer answers what can cross — a bound set by the two beams' phase space, with no conversion in it. realise then asks what the receiving lens's own conversion actually delivers against that bound.

realised  =  ideal × efficiency

receiving lensidealrealised efficiencyshortfall
a faithful inverse727.3968727.39681.00000.0000
an inverse that halves the surface727.3968181.84920.2500545.5476

A faithful inverse reaches the bound exactly — efficiency 1.0000. Halving the surface on the way back gives 0.25, and the missing 545.5 belongs to the lens, not to the geometry.

That split is the whole reason the two reads are separate. Energy that cannot cross because the phase space forbids it is physics and no design will recover it. Energy lost in the conversion is engineering, and the library names the nonimaging methods that exist to remove it — CPC, flow-line, SMS.

Efficiency above 1 is not an error condition. It is reported as measured, and it means the registered conversion is active — it put out more than it took in.

The check that passes for free

balance asks whether each side actually settles at the zero it declared. It reports a residual and a p-value, and closed where the remainder is what a balanced side would show anyway.

Read the first row carefully. Under the derived zero — the column mean — the residual is 9.1e-17 and the side reads closed. It could not have read anything else. The column mean is defined as the point where the column mean is zero, so the check is comparing a quantity against its own definition, and the answer is arithmetic.

The library says so plainly: under the default zero the residual is 0 by construction and every side reads closed; “a side that declares its OWN zero is genuinely tested.” Only the second row is a measurement.

Both rows say closed. One of them means something. A green result whose mechanism cannot produce a red one is arithmetic wearing a check's clothes, and the only defence is knowing which kind you are looking at.

What a lens is, at the end

A name, an entry, and optionally an inverse. Everything that makes it trustworthy is measured, not declared:

readanswers
linearmodes convert independently — which is what lets one lens serve a beam of any width
certifythe round trip loses nothing the instrument could see
balancethe side settles where it says it does
realisethe conversion reaches the bound the phase space permits

None of the four is a threshold anyone typed. Each puts a leftover back on a screen and reads it with the same instrument the rest of the course has been using — which is why a lens is judged, in the end, by the reader that will read through it.

Three things on these slides are worth carrying forward, and none of them is a number. A certificate can pass vacuously (the derived zero). A certificate can pass partially and say so in a field beside the verdict (modes). And a check that looks redundant can be load-bearing, because each half catches a family the other cannot see.

Where this goes next. Unit B2 separates three reads that all sound like “how well does this survive the lens?” and are not the same question — projection, comparison, transfer. Unit B4 puts two lenses in series and asks which of the four certificates above survive being chained and which compound, which is the first thing you need if a lens is ever going to sit behind another one.
