Entroptics / lesson six · the crossing

Two Screens
Meeting

Every unit so far has read one record. One grid, one spectrum, one floor, one answer. This is the first unit about two — and it is where the optical language stops being a helpful picture and starts being the actual arithmetic.

Two signals only meet if something carries them into the same coordinates. That something is a lens. What they land on is a screen. And the interesting questions are all about the crossing.

Both sides are real, and both are records you have already met.

What a lens is

A lens is not a number and it holds no data. It is a small bundle of one side's own laws — how that side converts its surface into the shared coordinates, and how it measures itself:

the lens carrieswhat it isif you leave it out
entrythis side's surface → the shared framerequired — no lens without it
inversethe shared frame → back to this side's surfacethe side is entry-only: it can be placed, read and coupled, but never rendered back
energythis side's own idea of energy per stepthe screen uses the sum of squares
zerowhere this side balances, by its own lawthe screen subtracts the column mean

Two things a lens must respect. Every side must land on the same width — place one that does not and the call raises, naming the width the screen holds.

And a lens is not allowed to declare its own phase space. That is measured off the frame by the screen, deliberately: a side that could declare its own phase space could claim more brightness than it was sent. The rule exists so the brightness accounting this unit closes on cannot be gamed.

The two sides

Split the EEG down the middle. Electrodes 1 and 2 become one side; electrodes 3 and 4 become the other. Same 800 samples, same clock, same recording — two instruments looking at one thing.

This is the honest version of the question. We are not asking whether two unrelated datasets match. We are asking whether two views of the same subject resolve the same structure — which is what every sensor pair, every pair of cameras, and every second opinion is really asking.

Total energy is 1595.3 on the left and 1598.0 on the right. Nearly equal, which is what you would hope for from four electrodes on one head.

Do they see the same thing?

The screen answers with the same kind of number unit A4 used, against the same kind of null — but the shuffle is different in an important way. Here you re-pair the two sides: keep each side's own internal structure completely intact, and randomise only which row of one lines up with which row of the other.

That isolates exactly one question. Not does either side have structure — A4 already answered that — but are they structured together?

a signed number between −1 and +1, and invariant to either side's units — a side changing from volts to millivolts cannot move it. The z is invariant too, which matters more: the verdict rests on it.

So: yes, and weakly. The two halves of the head are genuinely coupled, but only about a tenth of the way toward moving as one. The z-score says the coupling is real; the strength says it is small. Those are different claims and the library reports them separately.

The control

Same move as unit A4. Shuffle the rows of one side only. Every number on both sides survives; all that is destroyed is which left-row sits beside which right-row.

From +4.117 to +0.708 — under the cutoff, nothing found.

And look at what happens to the strength. It returns exactly 0.0, and the read reports resolved = False.

That zero is exact, and it is the result of the comparison. A strength below the floor is reported as zero, because a small non-zero number invites you to believe in a weak effect, and there is no effect here at all. A returned zero says nothing above the floor.

One shuffle is one draw, and a null is a population. That was the whole of this slide until 2026-08-28 — a single re-pairing, its z reported as though it were the value a shuffle gives. Unit A7 is the argument for why that is not enough. So it was run 200 times, which takes a fraction of a second:

The recorded pair stands above every one of the 200. That is a much stronger statement than a comparison against one number, and it is the statement unit A4 makes properly on a single record: not “higher than a shuffle” but outside the whole range shuffling can produce.

And the zero is a rate, not a rule. Strength comes back exactly zero in 192 of 200 shuffles — the other 8 resolve, at up to 0.0604. Zero almost always and not quite always is what a threshold gives; saying it returns zero full stop would be describing one draw again.

Coupling is not order

Now the same test on the market, split four companies against the other four — the same grid unit A1 opened with.

The monthly changes couple at +5.181, strength 0.3828. They move together, clearly and measurably.

But unit A4 measured those same monthly changes and found no order at all — coherence z = +0.127, indistinguishable from noise. Both readings are correct and they are not in tension, because they ask different questions:

  • Order asks: does this month tell me about next month? No.
  • Coupling asks: does this month's move here tell me about this month's move there? Yes.

Eight technology companies rise and fall together in the same month, and that is real. It does not make any of them predictable from their own past. Moving together and being forecastable are separate properties, and conflating them is how people lose money.

Prices couple harder still, at +8.089 with strength 0.8189 — and for the same dull reason as in A4: price levels drift slowly, so both sides are near where they were.

What actually arrives

Coupling says the two sides are related. It does not say how much gets across. Send the left side's beam at the right side and the screen accounts for every unit of energy, in four parts:

partenergywhat it is
energy in1595.32everything the left side carries
participation0.4613the share of it lying in what the right side can resolve
pertinent735.96that share, in energy — the part with somewhere to land
τ0.9884how much room the receiver has, relative to the sender
delivered727.40pertinent × τ. What arrives
reflected8.57coupled, but no room. Turned back
bystanding859.36never coupled at all — and retrievable, as a frame you can put on another screen

τ is the Greek letter tau, said TOW to rhyme with “now”. Here it is the ratio of the receiver's phase space to the sender's, capped at 1 — you cannot gain room by being sent to.

The three parts sum to the energy in, exactly: 727.397 + 8.567 + 859.358 = 1595.322. Not approximately — the check runs at zero difference. Nothing is lost at a crossing and nothing is invented; loss belongs to the travel between crossings, where it is charged against a measured distance.

And it runs one way

Same two sides. Same screen. Same instant. Left to right delivers 727.40. Right to left delivers 0.00.

The reason is on the screen already. The right side resolves 1 mode; the left side resolves 0. A beam can only land on what the receiver can resolve, so left→right has a target and right→left has none — its participation is 0.0 and the entire 1597.99 passes through as bystanding.

Nothing is lost in that direction, and nothing is delivered either. The energy is still there, still retrievable, still available to any screen that can resolve it. A crossing that delivers nothing is not the same as a crossing that destroys something — and most systems that get this wrong get it wrong by treating them as the same.

What a crossing cannot manufacture

There is one thing the accounting will not let you do, and it is the reason the lens was forbidden to declare its own phase space, back where a lens was defined.

Radiance is energy per unit of phase space — brightness, in the ordinary optical sense. Across any crossing:

radiance out  ≤  radiance in

≤ is less than or equal to. On this crossing the two are equal to the last digit — in and out both 590,537 — because the receiver's phase space is slightly the smaller of the two, and equality holds exactly when a crossing concentrates.

Sent the other way it would fall, by exactly the ratio of the two phase spaces and by no other amount. You can concentrate a beam for free; you cannot dilute one and keep it bright.

This is the optical statement of a rule the rest of the platform enforces in a different currency: composition along a path is monotone and non-amplifying. No lens, no screen and no crossing anywhere in the construction makes something brighter than what it was handed. It is checked as a theorem, not as a test.

Three things worth carrying out of here:

  • Two records meet only in shared coordinates. Everything else follows from getting them onto one screen — and a side of another width raises, naming the width it wanted.
  • Coupled, ordered and delivered are three different questions. The market's changes are coupled and not ordered. The EEG's right side is coupled and receives nothing when it transmits. None of these contradict.
  • The accounting closes exactly, and the ceiling is a theorem. Energy in equals delivered plus reflected plus bystanding, at zero difference; radiance out never exceeds radiance in.

That closes the optical core — six units on the instrument itself. What follows is the physics between: how a lens works in the system, how records are compared and transferred, and what propagation and attenuation actually cost.
