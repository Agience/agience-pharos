Two Verdicts

Unit D5g put every record against a ceiling. Unit A4 asks the same records a different question. Both answers are correct, and on three of eight records they point opposite ways.

Both lines are this course's own. The first is the counting floor of unit D5a exponentiated; its reciprocal is 0.759836, the number unit D5e writes on line 7 of the whole calculation. The second is the level unit A4 settled on and units A13 and B5 have used since.

The two verdicts line up on 5 records and cross on 3. Lining up means the record sits under the ceiling and reads as nothing, or sits over it and reads as order. The highlighted rows are the ones where that correspondence breaks.

The rows column matters for the second verdict. Coherence looks along the record, so the scan's reading comes from 8 rows and the EEG's from 800. A verdict from eight rows carries what eight rows can carry.

Contrast has no such column to watch: unit D5g measured that shuffling the rows leaves it unchanged to sixteen decimal places, so how many rows there are and what order they come in are outside what it reads.

The EEG sits under the ceiling and reads +41.287 on coherence, which is the strongest order reading in this course. One test places it with the quiet records and the other puts it at the top of the ordered ones.

The market's monthly changes go the other way: 2.37412 puts them over the ceiling, and -1.119 puts them with the noise. Unit D5g worked out what that combination is — eight stocks moving together within one month gives a dominant pattern across the columns, and the months arriving in any order is what coherence reports.

Both readings on both records are correct. They are answers to two questions, and a record can answer one way on the first and the other way on the second.

Why crossing is what to expect

Unit D5g measured this directly. Take a record, put its rows in a random order twenty times, and read both:

readafter shuffling the rows what that means it uses
contrastidentical, to sixteen decimal places the columns and their relationships
coherencecollapses from its high reading to near zero the order the rows arrive in

So the two reads use different parts of the record. Reordering rows is a rigid motion of the numbers, so the size of the largest pattern survives it exactly; the row order is gone, and the read that asks about row order has nothing left.

Two reads that use different parts of a record will sort records differently. Three crossings out of eight is that fact arriving on real data. A run where the two agreed everywhere would mean the eight records happened to be simple ones.

This is the same lesson unit A11 opens with and unit C3 works out on two populations: what a number means is fixed by what the read looks at, and two correct numbers about one record can point different ways.

The paper says this of its own comparison. Its section 12 records that the margin reads finite on both sides of the transition it is applied to: the comparison holds in both phases, so passing it settles something other than which phase a configuration is in. That is the same shape of result as the three crossings above, and this page is that shape measured on records a reader can hold.

What to do with a test whose verdict is this. Use it for what it settles and bring a second read for the rest. Unit D5g's comparison against the counting floor is exact and worth having; the EEG passing it at 1.25157 while reading +41.287 on coherence is two facts about the EEG, and each needs its own measurement.

Every number on these five slides is measured here, on the eight records unit D8 describes. No figure from the paper is quoted, because the point being made is about the shape of a result and this course states figures it has taken itself.
