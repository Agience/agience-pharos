Two Populations in One Record

Every read in this course returns a number about a record. This unit is about a record that is two things — where the obvious comparison is available, and wrong, and the control that fixes it costs one line.

the record
what it isground height in metres, on a grid that crosses a coastline
shape91 rows × 120 columns
values-1437 m (sea floor) to 2205 m (mountain)
above sea level55.6% of its cells

So some rows are mostly land and some are mostly sea. Split them on that, count the separable spots unit B5 measures, and here is the table anyone would build first:

rowsspots
the whole grid9129.36
mostly-land rows5923.64
mostly-sea rows319.84

Read straight down, that says the land is finer than the sea and the whole grid beats either part. The second is false and the first is unearned — and the reason is in the middle column.

The three rows are three different sizes

91 rows, 59 rows, 31 rows. Unit A11's first test is that a read moves with how much record it is given, so a table whose rows differ in size is a table that has not asked its question yet.

The control is one line: draw the same number of rows at random from the whole grid, and see what a slice of that size reads. 12 draws each:

rowsobserved a random slice of that sizez
mostly-land5923.64 16.81 to 20.61+4.12outside
mostly-sea319.84 8.08 to 11.03+0.04inside

The land sits outside the band; the sea sits on its mean. 23.64 against a band running 16.81 to 20.61 is +4.12 standard deviations out. And 9.84 against 8.08 to 11.03 is +0.04 — not near the middle of what a random slice gives, but on it.

Which is the finding, and it is not the one the first table suggested. The land carries structure its size does not explain. The sea floor reads exactly what any 31 rows of this grid read.

And the whole grid was never beating either part

The first table's other reading was that 91 rows of everything resolve 29.36 spots where 59 rows of land resolve 23.64 — so pooling the two populations found something neither had.

It did not. A random 59 rows of this grid resolve 16.81 to 20.61, and 31 rows resolve 9.84. More rows resolve more spots, whatever is in them, which is the same thing unit A11 measures on the EEG and unit E3 on terrain.

91 rows → 29.36  ·  59 rows → 16.81 to 20.61  ·  31 rows → 8.08 to 11.03

So the pooled number carries no finding at all. It is the biggest of the three because it is the biggest slice of the three, and a reader who quotes it as the grid's resolution is quoting a fact about how many rows the file happens to have.

Pooling can work the other way too: mix a small population into a large one and the average reports the majority, so what the minority shows is diluted away. Here pooling inflates, for a different reason — more record. Both are the same instruction, and it is unit A11's: say what population a number is about, and match it before comparing.

What the control bought

the readingbefore the control after it
the land is finer than the sea23.64 against 9.84 true, and now earned: +4.12 out of its own size's band
the whole grid beats either part29.36 against 23.64 false — 59 rows of anything reads 16.81 to 20.61
the sea floor is poorly resolved9.84, the lowest number it reads what its size reads: +0.04
  • One extra measurement changed two of three readings. Drawing the same number of rows at random costs a line and settles what the first table could not.
  • A z of +0.04 is a null worth having. The sea floor sits on the mean of its own size's band, so this unit says nothing about the sea floor except that its resolution is what its size gives.
  • Match before comparing. Two populations in one record differ in every way at once — how many rows, how rough, how deep. The control fixes the one that was doing the work.

What this unit establishes is that one of the two populations resolves more than its size explains. Why land should be rougher than sea floor at this scale is a question for somebody who studies coastlines, and it sits outside every measurement here.
