Entroptics / lesson twelve · the Jacobian lens

The Lens Inside
a Language Model

Everything in this programme so far has been an instrument looking at data. This unit points it at something else: the inside of a working language model, treated as an optical object.

No new mathematics. The lens from unit A6, the spectrum from unit A2, and one subtraction.

Measured here on a stored 388 MB transport for a 4-billion-parameter model, on this machine, in about a minute.

What is being measured

A language model builds its answer in layers. Something enters at layer 1, gets worked on, and what comes out the far end is the answer. The natural question is: if I nudge what is happening at layer 12, how does that show up at the end?

You can answer it with a big square table. Down the side, every direction you could nudge; across the top, every direction the nudge could arrive in; and in each cell, how much. Averaged over a lot of real text, that table is one number per pair — here 2,560 × 2,560 of them.

That table is the transport, and it is exactly the kind of object units A1 to A6 know how to read. It is a lens: it carries something from one place into another set of coordinates.

Everything below reads that table as geometry. Nothing here trains anything, runs the model, or looks at what any direction “means”.

One number for “how many directions”

Any such table can be summarised as a row of bars, tallest first — like a graphic equaliser. A tall bar is a direction the transport really uses; a short one barely matters.

Counting the bars that are “up” needs no cutoff. Instead ask: if all the energy were spread evenly over some number of bars, how many would that be? That number is the effective rank.

effective rank  =  (sum of s²)²  ÷  sum of s⁴

It is honest in the way that matters: for exactly r equal bars it returns exactly r, and there is no threshold anywhere in it. One tall bar and a thousand tiny ones gives a number near 1, which is what you would want.

Same idea as counting modes above a noise floor in units A1 and A2, without needing a floor.

Something is in the way

Before measuring anything, one thing can be predicted. These models carry a running total: each layer adds its contribution to what came before. So whatever a late layer does, most of what arrives at the end is the running total simply being passed along.

In the transport that shows up as a “do nothing” component — and it should grow with depth, because there is less left to do above. Measured:

layer06121824262830
“do nothing” share0.00800.02610.07030.23570.58320.62940.70590.7896

From 0.0080 to 0.7896. By the last layer nearly four-fifths of the transport is just passing the total along.

So it has to come off before anything else can be seen — and how you remove it matters. It is not fitted. There is exactly one right amount to subtract, the same way there is exactly one right shadow to remove, and taking it out leaves a table whose diagonal sums to 1.1e-13 at layer 30 — zero, to the machine's precision. Nothing was tuned. That is one layer; the closing slide reports the same subtraction at every layer in the table above, which is what a claim about an operation requires.

The whole finding, in three rows

layer06121824262830
“do nothing” share0.00800.02610.07030.23570.58320.62940.70590.7896
effective rank of the transport12.469.388.7240.3987.01115.21249.11328.5
…with pass-through removed12.367.880.3163.2326.1345.0262.6124.4

Row two climbs to 1328.5 and stops there, at the last layer. Read on its own it says only “deeper is bigger”, which is what the pass-through does. No story.

Row three rises to 345.0 at layer 26 and then collapses to 124.4. A band — somewhere around four-fifths of the way up, the transport resolves far more directions than it does at either end.

This is the rarest thing in a measurement: the control is inside the figure. Rows two and three are the same read of the same object, differing by one subtraction, and row one says exactly what was subtracted and how much. Nobody has to be trusted about the method — the comparison is right there.

A number that could not have been right

Two mistakes can be made in building that table, and neither raises an error. Made together, they produce this:

layer“do nothing” effective rank
00.00004237.8
300.0002166672.3

Both columns are wrong, for two different reasons. The share was already a share and was divided by the total a second time. The effective-rank function takes a row of bar heights and was handed the whole table; it returned a number for that too.

The tell is the second column. An effective rank counts directions, so it cannot exceed the number of directions there are — 2,560. A reading of 166,672 is an impossible one, and an impossible result is the cheapest kind to catch. A plausible-looking 300 would have passed.

The habit worth copying: before believing a number, ask what range it is even allowed to be in. Most broken measurements announce themselves that way, and only that way.

Two summary statements, measured

The work above is published with a summary. Two of that summary's statements were checked directly against the same file that produced everything else, and the file reads differently.

The finding stands on either reading: the band is there, the peak is at the same layer, and the pass-through removal is exact. What moves is the summary's two figures.

And one of the two was already corrected by the project itself. Its own findings file carries the corrected number. The summary, the paper, and three separate places in the source carry the earlier one — a correction made in one place out of five.

A transport is a table, and a table has a spectrum

A language model is not usually described as an optical system, and nothing here needed it to be one. A transport is a table; a table has a spectrum; a spectrum has an effective rank; and one component of the table was predictable from the architecture before anything was measured.

  • The structure was invisible until something was removed. Effective rank as measured says nothing. With the pass-through taken out it peaks at 345.0 around layer 26 and collapses at both ends.
  • The removal is exact, not fitted — and that is a claim about the operation, so it is checked at every layer. The corrected table's diagonal sums to exactly zero at 5 of the 8 layers; the worst of the rest is 2.3e-13, at layer 24, against the 2654.2373 the removal cancelled there — a relative size of 9e-17, which is smaller than the 2.2e-16 that double-precision arithmetic can resolve at all. The projection is exact to the last bit the machine has.
  • The control sits inside the figure, so the method does not have to be trusted separately from the result.

And the lesson that outlives the numbers: this project lints its own prose and records its own corrections, and a corrected number still stands in four other places. A correction travels only as far as something carries it. Every figure on these pages is re-derived from the live code on every build for exactly that reason.
