Entroptics / lesson A20 · when splitting cannot work

More Than One Change

Unit A19 finds where a record changed by scanning split points and taking the first jump. Its closing slide names the limit: a record with two changes is not something it measures. Here is that record.

900 rows, decaying at 0.05 for 300 of them, then at 1.50 for 300, then at 0.05 again. Two changes, at rows 300 and 600.

Run A19's scan on it. Every split point, the ratio between the parts each time:

every ratio between 1.22 and 2.75  ·  no jump anywhere

Not less accurate — nothing at all. The record reads exactly like one that never changed, and A19's rule has no answer to give: no split separates the two regimes, so no ratio trebles.

An empty answer is the useful failure here. A method that reported row 300 would send a reader to a place nothing happened, and this one sends them nowhere. The next slide is what makes it so.

Every split has a mixture on both sides

Split this record anywhere and ask what is on each side of the cut:

split atbefore itafter it
150slowfast, then slow
450slow, then fastfast, then slow
750slow, then fastslow

There is no cut that puts one regime on the left and the other on the right. A19's method works by finding such a cut, and on this record none exists — so the ratio stays near one wherever the split goes, and the record is indistinguishable from one that never changed.

The problem is in the shape of the question. Split it in two and compare the parts assumes the record has two parts. This one has three.

And the general case is worse: a record with any even number of changes is balanced this way. Changing and changing back looks exactly like never changing, to any method that only ever cuts once.

Stop splitting. Slide.

The three lying wholly between rows 300 and 600 read 3.01, 2.39, 2.39, against 22.74 and above for every window that does not — including the one starting at 525, which straddles the second change and reads with the slow stretch because half of it is slow.

So the windows say where both changes are — the reading drops at the first and comes back at the second, and a reader can point at the rows.

The record below it is the control: one decay rate throughout, and its windows sit between 6.94 and 12.34. Nothing to point at, which is correct.

The EEG spreads by 4.39 — wider than any stationary record this unit built, and it has no changes in it. So the margin this table really offers is 4.39 against 15.6, not 2.6 against 15.6.

A line drawn from built records alone would have been drawn at about three, and the EEG would have crossed it. Draw it against the noisiest record you actually expect — which is unit A16's instruction in a different costume.

What sliding costs, and what it buys

  • A19's method returns nothing here, and that is the right failure. Every ratio between 1.22 and 2.75, no jump. A record that changes and changes back is balanced across every possible cut.
  • Windows locate both changes. The reading drops entering the fast stretch and comes back leaving it, so a reader can point at rows 300 and 600.
  • A window resolves less than the record. Every number on the sliding table is a reading of 150 rows, not 900. Detail below what 150 rows can carry is gone before the comparison starts.
  • The window length is a choice. 150 rows and a step of 75 were picked so no stretch goes unlooked-at. A different length gives different numbers, and unit A11's first test is what to do about that.
