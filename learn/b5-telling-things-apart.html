Entroptics / lesson B5 · the diffraction limit

Telling Things Apart

The oldest number in optics is the one that says how close two things can be and still arrive as two. Closer than that and they arrive as one, and no amount of care afterwards separates them again. This unit measures it on a record.

aδ = 1 ÷ 2H   ·   spots = 1 ÷ aδ = 2H

You have already built this. Unit A12 took the power share across the columns, measured its entropy in bits, and raised two to it to get the number of columns carrying the record. This is the same three steps on a different subject: the entropy is of the record's own decay, and 2H counts how many things along it can be told apart.

The split is not gradual. Every record with order in it resolves at least 10.81 spots. Every record with nothing in it resolves under 2.93. There is nothing between 2.93 and 10.81 on this table.

And the two columns agree without being connected. The coherence read looks along the ordered axis and asks whether the row order matters; the spot count looks at the decay spectrum and asks how finely the record can be divided. They are different measurements and they sort the eight records the same way.

The market's monthly changes resolve 2.06 spots, which puts them inside the noise band. That is the same answer units A3, A4 and A5 reach by three other routes, and this is a fourth.

The floor, and the four records that establish it

The limit carries a second number, the shape factor ξ, and it is bounded below by 1. A record with nothing to resolve sits on that floor:

record with nothing in itξspots

3 of the 4 sit at exactly 1 — not near it, but 1 to every digit a double carries. The remaining one reads 1.0089.

Which is what the fourth draw establishes. With only the market's changes and a single noise draw, every unstructured record on the table would have read exactly 1, and the honest description of that is a constant. A fourth draw shows it is a floor, and the difference matters: a constant that turns out to vary is a broken instrument, and a floor that is reached is an instrument saying there is nothing here.

The same bytes, told apart twice

The EEG file is 3,200 numbers with nothing in it saying how they are laid out. Read them as 4 columns and as 8 — the same bytes, no value changed:

shapeH bitsaδ spots
800 × 44.5691850.042125 23.74
400 × 83.4338720.09253410.81

Reading them eight at a time more than halves what can be told apart: 23.74 spots becomes 10.81. Both readings are correct arithmetic on the record they were given, and they answer different questions — unit D8 reads the same file at six shapes and unit E3 reads a terrain grid in two directions, both reaching this from their own side.

So the resolution belongs to the reading, not to the file. A spot count quoted without the shape it was read at is a number with its question removed, which is the same rule unit A11 arrives at for every read in this course.

This is the practical use of the limit. Before asking whether two things in a record are distinguishable, count the spots the reading resolves. If the answer is 23.74, then 23.74 is how many separable things the record can hold, and looking for more of them is looking for something the reading cannot carry.

  • It is A12's arithmetic on a different subject. Entropy of a share, then two to the power of it. A12 counts columns carrying the record; this counts things that can be told apart.
  • The split is clean. Order resolves 10.81 spots or more; nothing resolves under 2.93. Two measurements built on different quantities sort eight records the same way.
  • ξ is a floor at 1, and it is reached. 3 of 4 records with nothing in them sit exactly on it, and the fourth reads 1.0089 — which is why the slide says floor.
  • The reading owns the resolution. The same bytes resolve 23.74 spots at four columns and 10.81 at eight.

Three relations the instrument documents were checked before this deck was written and all three hold: aδ = 1/2H, shape_factor = aδ/φF — where φ is the Greek letter phi, unit A12's fill fraction — and rayleigh_shape_factor = ξ·aδ. The audit re-checks them on every build, because a formula in a docstring is a claim and the value beside it is the measurement.
