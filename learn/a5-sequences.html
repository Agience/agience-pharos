An Ordered Axis
With No Numbers

Units one to three read a grid of measurements — a spectrum, a floor, a count of modes that stand above it. This one reads a record that has no measurements in it at all: a row of symbols. Letters. Up, flat, down. There is nothing to take a square root of.

The question changes with the data. Not how many modes stand above the floor, but: how much of the next symbol does the past already fix?

Two records, carried the whole way down, as in every unit before this one.

Counting one symbol at a time

Start with the simplest question: if I show you one symbol from the record, how surprised should you be? Count how often each symbol appears, turn the counts into shares, and add up one term per symbol.

H1  =  − Σ p × log2 p

Σ is the Greek capital letter sigma. It is said SIG-ma, and it means add up everything that follows — one term for each symbol in the alphabet, all summed. H is the entropy, measured in bits. p is one symbol's share of the record, a number between 0 and 1. log2 asks “two to what power?”

A bit is a yes/no question. H1 is how many yes/no questions you would need, on average, to pin down one symbol if you knew nothing but how common each one is.

record A

4.126 bits

of a possible log2 28 = 4.807, if all 28 symbols were equally common. They are not — e and the space are everywhere, q and z are not.

record B

1.520 bits

of a possible log2 3 = 1.585. Close to the ceiling: the three months are used evenly.

Now look at blocks

One symbol at a time throws away everything. Real records have context: after t h, the next letter is very often e. So count blocks of length n, and get Hn — the entropy of a whole block.

The useful number is the difference between consecutive blocks:

hn  =  Hn+1  −  Hn

That is how much the next symbol still costs you once you already know the n before it — the surprise the past has not already paid for. Small hn means the past nearly determines what comes next.

Read it off the table below and the subtraction is visible: the n = 1 row carries H = 4.126, the n = 2 row carries 7.525, and the difference — 3.399 — is the h on the first row. n is a length of context, so hn is always the cost of the symbol after it.

English gives itself away

From 3.399 bits with one letter of context down to 0.177 bits with 8. By eight letters of context the next letter is nearly determined — you are paying about a fifth of one yes/no question for it.

This is Claude Shannon's 1951 experiment, reproduced by the library on a file you can open. He got it by asking people to guess the next letter; here it is counted.

And so does the market…?

From 1.482 down to -0.023 bits. Steeper than English, in fact — it finishes closer to zero. Read this chart the way we just read the last one and the conclusion is obvious: the market is even more predictable than English.

It is not. This chart is an artefact, and the next slide is how you find that out without knowing anything about markets.

Shuffle it and see

Take the record and shuffle the symbols into a random order. Every symbol still appears exactly as often as before — only the order is destroyed. Then measure the block entropies again, 200 times, and ask: is the real record's Hn lower than a shuffled one's?

If order carries information, the real blocks repeat and Hn comes in below the shuffled average. If it does not, the two agree.

Why record B's fall was fake

Count the blocks. Record B has 70 symbols, so it contains 63 blocks of length 8. The number of blocks of length 8 that could exist is

38  =  6,561

You are trying to estimate how often each of 6,561 possible blocks occurs, from 63 examples. Almost every block appears once or not at all — so every block looks unique, and “unique” measures as “perfectly predictable from its predecessor”.

The estimator ran out of data, and a shortage of data reads exactly like structure. That is why the shuffled control matters: the shuffled record has the same shortage, so it falls the same way, and the difference between them is zero.

English survives the same test only because 17404 characters is enough to see a block of 8 more than once. Same instrument, same threshold, different amount of evidence.

Two numbers that survive the test

Redundancy is the share of the record that its own past already accounts for. English: 0.2753 — better than a quarter of the text is not carrying anything new. The market's direction: 0.0000, exactly zero, because nothing survived the control.

Turn that into a length. Effective length is how long the record would be if every symbol carried its full share:

record A

12,612 of 17,404

4,792 symbols of English are, in the strict sense, already implied by their neighbours.

record B

70 of 70

Nothing is implied by anything. Every month is its own news.

This is the same sentence units one to three ended on, reached without a single measurement: the record is narrower than it looks — when it is.

Two routes to the same “no structure”

The instrument answered “no structure” for the market's direction, and that agrees with what unit A4 found on the same prices by a completely different route: the coherence z-score of the monthly returns is +0.13, indistinguishable from noise, while the price levels read +9.30.

Two instruments — one spectral, one symbolic — that share no code path, reaching the same verdict on the same record. That agreement is worth more than either reading alone.

One row is not like the others, and it is worth the space. At 0.5% the surrogate test reports an onset at block 8. That is the narrowest band, where the flat symbol has nearly gone — 9 of 70 months — so the alphabet is close to two symbols and the block counts are at their thinnest. Redundancy still reads zero there, because the control falls with it. The onset is reported, and reporting it is the point: this is the unit that exists to say a falling curve becomes a finding once the shuffle has stayed put.
