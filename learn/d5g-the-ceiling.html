The Ceiling

The whole argument comes down to one comparison between two numbers. Unit D5a worked out the first of them by counting. This page measures the second on records you already have and puts them side by side.

μ < κ0    which is the same as    contrast < 1.316074

Reading the two letters. κ, said kappa, is the counting floor from unit D5a. μ, said mu, is what the record's own leading mode is worth. σ, said sigma, will appear on the fourth slide as the size of a mode.

Two counting quantities and a < sign. Everything on the next six slides is that comparison being made, and the arithmetic on both sides is something a reader can repeat.

The floor, turned into a ceiling

Unit D5a counts the ways a path can run along the edges of a cube and arrives at a floor of one quarter of the natural logarithm of 3:

κ0 = ¼ × ln 3 = 0.274653

The comparison in the paper is written with logarithms on both sides. Raising e to each side turns it into a comparison of ordinary numbers, and the ceiling that comes out has a much simpler name:

written this wayvalue
e raised to κ01.316074
the fourth root of 31.316074

The same number, to every decimal. Taking a quarter of a logarithm and then undoing the logarithm leaves a fourth root, so the ceiling is 3¼ — a number you can get on any calculator by typing 3, then square root, then square root again.

That is worth doing once by hand. The square root of 3 is about 1.732, and the square root of that is 1.316074. Everything on this slide is a counting constant and a root.

Measuring the other side

The ceiling is a real dividing line on ordinary data. It was worked out by counting paths on a cube, before any record was measured, and it lands in the middle of the range these eight records occupy — between 0.94660 and 7.02379.

A record can sit either side of it. The next slide is about what putting a record on one side actually tells you, because two different questions are easy to confuse here and this course has spent several units on the difference.

Shuffle the rows and watch which number moves

Take a record, put its rows in a random order 20 times, and measure both reads each time. Shuffling rows destroys any order along the record and leaves every column's relationship to the others exactly as it was:

the EEG1.251571.25157 to 1.25157+41.287-1.438 to +3.246

Contrast comes back identical to every digit shown. Across 20 shuffles the EEG's spread is 6.7e-16, which is the smallest difference the arithmetic can represent. The coherence read on the same record goes from +41.287 to between -1.438 and +3.246.

This is a fact about the arithmetic, and the table above is it happening. Reordering the rows of a table is a rigid motion of the numbers in it: every length survives it, so the size of the largest mode is exactly what it was. The row order carries no weight in that measurement, and coherence is the read that asks about order.

So the two answer different questions. The market's monthly changes are 2.37412 on contrast, above the ceiling, and -1.119 on coherence, which sits inside the noise band. Eight stocks moving together within the same month gives one dominant mode across the columns; the months themselves arriving in any order is what coherence reports. Units A11 and C3 are about exactly this distinction.

What was done here. The paper's comparison was rewritten into a ratio, the ratio was measured on eight records, and both forms of the comparison were checked against each other. The arithmetic is complete and a reader can repeat every line of it.

Where it stops. The paper makes this comparison on a gauge screen as the screen is refined, and that is a statement about a field. An EEG landing under the ceiling says what that one record's leading mode is worth against a counting constant, and that is the whole of what it says. These eight records are here so the inequality becomes something a reader has evaluated with their own hands.

Where the pieces sit. Unit D5a supplies the floor, D5f says which theories the argument covers, and units B6 and B7 measure what a finite opening does to a decay. This page is the comparison those four are for.

The ceiling is 3¼. Three, square root, square root: 1.316074.
