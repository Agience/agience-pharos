One Read, Two Names

A physicist and this course have been measuring the same things under different names. This page is the list, with the numbers, on one record.

Reading the letters. μ, said mu, and α, said alpha, are two ways of asking how much the record's largest pattern stands out. κ, said kappa, is a counting constant. Δ, said delta, is the gap itself.

They come out identical on 5 of the eight records and differ on 3. The highlighted rows are the identical ones, and their difference column reads 0.000000 — not close to zero, exactly zero.

Look at the second column to see which rows those are. Every record where the two agree has 1 pattern above the edge, or none. Every record where they differ has 2 or more.

Why that is exactly the rule

α takes whichever is larger, the second pattern or the edge. So the question is which of those two wins, and the count in the middle column answers it:

patterns above the edgewhere the second pattern sitswhat α divides byso α and μ are
1, or nonebelow the edgethe edge one number
2 or moreabove the edgethe second pattern two numbers

With one pattern above the edge, the second one is below it by definition. That is what "one above the edge" means. So α divides by the edge, which is what μ does, and the two calculations are the same calculation.

the terrain grid is the clearest case of the other kind. It has 15 patterns above the edge, its second pattern reads 165.2473 against an edge of 37.7847, and the two attenuations come out 1.475540 apart.

What each one is good for. μ asks how far the record stands above nothing at all, which is the comparison unit D5g makes against the counting floor. α asks how far the leading pattern stands above its nearest rival, which is a question about whether one pattern dominates. On a record with a single pattern those questions have one answer, and the table above is that fact measured.

the scan is one of the agreeing records: 1 above the edge, and both attenuations read 0.231767.
