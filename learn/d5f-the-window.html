The Window

Units D5 to D5e work out a gap for one theory. This page decides which theories that argument covers, and the whole decision is two numbers you can work out on paper.

b0 = 11N÷3 − 2Nf÷3   ·   b1 = 34N2÷3 − (10N÷3 + (N2−1)÷N) Nf

Reading the two letters. N is how many colours the theory has — three, for the one the paper is about. Nf is how many kinds of matter are in it. Everything below is these two formulas evaluated at whole numbers.

The first number: does the force grow?

Start with b0 at three colours. With no matter it is 11, and every kind of matter you add takes 2/3 off it:

It is a straight line, and it crosses zero at Nf = 33/2, which is 16.5000. Below that crossing b0 is positive and the force between two colour charges grows as they move apart. Above it, the force fades with distance the way an ordinary electric force does.

Growing with distance is the whole thing. Pull two charges apart and the energy between them keeps climbing, so the field gathers into a narrow tube running between them. Unit D5 calls that tube the screen, and this is the arithmetic that says a tube forms at all.

The crossing sits at a half, so no whole number of flavours lands on it. At Nf = 16 b0 is 1/3 and still positive; at 17 it is -1/3.

The second number: is there somewhere to stop?

A force that grows can grow forever, or it can settle at a fixed strength and stay there. The second coefficient decides which. At three colours it starts at 102 and every flavour takes 38/3 off it:

It crosses zero at Nf = 153/19, which is 8.0526. Look at the two highlighted rows: at 8 flavours b1 is 2/3, still above zero, and at 9 it is -12. The crossing lands between two whole numbers, and the fractions show it landing.

Once b1 is negative the growing has a place to stop. The first number pushes the strength up as you look at longer distances, the second pulls it back, and where they balance the strength stops changing. A theory whose force settles to a fixed strength looks the same at every scale, and unit B7 measured what that means for a decay: it is the ending with no gap.

Both numbers are straight lines in Nf, so the whole of this page is two lines and where each one crosses zero.

Both at once

Eight whole flavour counts, 9 through 16. A three-colour theory with that much matter in it looks the same at every scale and has no gap. This is the range physicists call the conformal window, and the two lines above are where it comes from.

This is the foil the argument needs. A claim that a finite screen forces a gap has to explain the theories that have none. These are they, and what puts them here is arithmetic on two coefficients.

Where the paper's theory sits

The theory units D5 to D5e work on has no matter at all: Nf = 0. Put that into both formulas, for four different colour counts:

Both numbers positive, every time. The force grows, and the growing has nowhere to stop. At three colours that is 11 and 102, and the window starts at 8.05 flavours — this theory sits a long way below it.

The other side of the comparison. Electromagnetism on its own has a coefficient of 0: its force stays the same strength at every distance, its field spreads out through space, and no tube forms. The photon has no gap, and this is why.

That 0 is quoted from a different calculation. The 11N÷3 term counts the field acting on itself, which is what a colour force does; an electromagnetic field passes through itself freely, so its arithmetic begins somewhere else and this page quotes the answer from there.

The chain, in one line. b0 positive makes the force grow with distance; a growing force gathers into a tube; a tube is a finite opening; and units B6 and B7 measured what a finite opening does — it gives a decay that falls at a fixed rate, and that rate times a shape constant is the gap.

What a reader can check. Both formulas are on the first slide and both take whole numbers. Work out b0 and b1 at three colours for any flavour count and compare against the tables here; the two crossings at 8.0526 and 16.5000 follow from the steps 2/3 and 38/3 a flavour.
