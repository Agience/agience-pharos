From Decay to Spectrum

The measurement returns a rate. The claim is about a Hamiltonian. This unit is the step between them, and it is where the track closes.

The symbols here, said aloud: ρ is rho, a magnitude across one cut; κ is kappa and Δ is delta. ≥ is greater than or equal, ≤ is less than or equal, ± is plus or minus, and ∞ is infinity.

The target, written out. H is the Hamiltonian, spec its set of energy levels, and the vacuum sits at zero:

spec H  ⊆  {0}  ∪  [Δ, ∞)

Which says: the vacuum, then nothing at all until Δ. That empty interval is the mass gap.

One cut settles every cut

The Euclidean lattice has a transfer operator: the object that advances the state by one step along the time axis. Correlations across n steps are that operator applied n times.

Reflection positivity is the property that makes the operator self-adjoint, which is the well-behaved case: its modes are independent of each other, and each one is simply multiplied by its own factor at every step. Repeating the step n times therefore multiplies by that factor n times, so the magnitude across n cuts is the magnitude across one, raised to the power n:

ρ′(n)  =  ρ′(1)n

That single line does the heavy lifting for the whole argument, and it is worth seeing why.

  • It makes a local bound global. Establish ρ′(1) < 1 once, at one plaquette, and every n follows by taking powers. There is nothing left to prove at larger separations.
  • It makes the gap uniform in volume. The bound has no volume in it, so a bigger lattice cannot weaken it — which is what "uniform in volume" means and what the continuum limit needs.
  • It turns a magnitude into a rate. A multiplier of ρ′(1) per step is a decay at rate −log ρ′(1), so the gap and the magnitude are the same fact.

Reflection positivity is a cited input here, from Osterwalder–Seiler. It appears in the formal development as wilson_reflection_positive, and the closing slide shows exactly which theorems lean on it.

The floor, written as a ceiling

D5a's counting gave a rate. A rate of at least κ0 per step is a multiplier of at most e to the minus that rate, so the same statement reads as a ceiling on the single-cut magnitude:

rate ≥ κ0  ⇔  magnitude ≤ e−κ0 = 3−¼ = 0.7598

So the single-plaquette statement to establish is ρ′(1) = mcell ≤ 0.7598, and reflection positivity carries it to every separation. That inequality is machine-checked at one cell.

Two reads, two jobs

The aperture read establishes that a mode decays: the magnitude sits below the ceiling and off the unit circle. That is its job, and the numbers below show exactly where its job ends and another read's begins.

The phase is Ksignal's to answer. Coulomb U(1), above its deconfinement transition, reads a finite aperture too: 0.410 at β = 1.70 and 0.379 at β = 2.50, both under the 0.7598 ceiling. Confined SU(2) at L = 32 reads 0.687, above both of them. So the two orderings run opposite ways: aperture magnitude and phase are independent readings, and each needs its own instrument.

So the deconfinement transition is carried by Ksignal, the order parameter of unit D5c, and the aperture read supplies something narrower and still load-bearing: that the SU(N) ensemble the theorem concerns satisfies the aperture condition, verified at runtime on that ensemble.

Two reads, two jobs. Ksignal answers which phase; the aperture margin answers is the mode decaying. Naming which question a number answers is what keeps a measurement from being asked to do somebody else's work.

The whole thing, on a calculator

Every quantity in this argument is a factor per step. The measurement gives one number — how much of a fluctuation survives one step along the time axis — and everything else follows by multiplying, dividing and taking a logarithm.

measured: 0.3588 survives each step

Two. Compare it against the ceiling unit D5a counted. A plaquette carries 3¼ = 1.3161 times as many shapes, so a step has to lose at least one part in that:

The measured step loses 64.1% where it needed to lose 24.0%. It clears, with room.

Three. Write the factor as a rate, which is what a gap is:

Δ  =  −ln(0.3588)  =  1.0250   against   κ0 = 0.2747

A positive number, and it clears the floor by 0.7503. That subtraction is the mass gap: the vacuum, then a clear interval, then everything else.

Nothing on this slide needs an eigenvalue. The operator finds the factor; the reader checks what the factor implies. Those are two different jobs, and only the first one needs the machinery.

The bridge: a decay becomes a spectrum

Now the last step. The transfer operator's spectrum sits inside a set the decay pins down: the vacuum at 1, and everything else below the ceiling the decay rate fixes.

spec T  ⊆  {1}  ∪  [ε, e−Δ]

The Hamiltonian is what turns a multiplier into an energy, and it is defined by taking the logarithm of the operator — the same relation as one mode's, applied to the whole operator at once:

H  =  −log T

Feeding the first line into the second sends 1 to 0 and sends the ceiling e−Δ to Δ, and −log is decreasing, so the interval between them is carried across intact:

spec H  ⊆  {0}  ∪  [Δ, ∞)

Which is the statement the track set out to reach: the vacuum, then a clear interval, then the rest of the spectrum.

In the formal development this is Reconstruction.hamiltonian, built as cfc (fun x => -Real.log x) T — the continuous functional calculus, which is what applies a function to an operator's spectrum. The step is hamiltonian_spectrum_ge_gap, and the assembled statement is hamiltonian_mass_gap.

This step imports no physics. ym_mass_gap_spectral carries 3 axioms, and they are the three foundational ones every Lean development uses: Classical.choice, Quot.sound, propext. The bridge from decay to spectrum is mathematics, and the footprint table says so.

The chain, end to end

Four units, one inequality, and every link measured or proved:

Δ  ≥  κ0 − μ(β)  >  0

  • κ0 = 0.274653, by counting (D5a). Directed cube-paths: three choices, four plaquettes. No coupling, no spacing.
  • μ(β) stays under it (D5b). A cited bound evaluated at strong coupling, a computed limit at weak coupling, a theorem with one measured input in between.
  • Both read off real fields (D5c). One reduction to a screen, an eigenvalue for the rate, a mode count for the phase.
  • The rate becomes a spectrum (D5d). Reflection positivity gives ρ′(n) = ρ′(1)n, the floor gives the single-cut ceiling 0.7598, and −log carries the interval to spec H ⊆ {0} ∪ [Δ, ∞).

In the development the gap is defined as that margin — LatticeYM.massGap M β := M.κ₀ − M.μ β in research/lean/MassGap/GapRate.lean — and massGap_pos_of_confinement is the one-line consequence: the tension under the floor is the positive gap.
