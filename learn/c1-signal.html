Entroptics / lesson nine · where meaning becomes a number

Signal

Every unit so far started with a grid of numbers already in hand. Somebody had to make that grid. This is the first unit about the machinery that does — the part of the system whose whole job is turning something that is not numbers into something the instrument can read.

The something here is words. And the striking part is what it does without: no model, no training, no learned weights, nothing fitted to a corpus. The geometry falls out of a dictionary's own shape.

Words already live in a tree

A dictionary of the right kind says which things are kinds of which other things. A dog is a kind of canine, which is a kind of carnivore, and so on up to entity, which everything is a kind of.

entity → physical entity → object → whole → living thing → organism
       → animal → chordate → vertebrate → mammal → placental
       → carnivore → canine → dog

The tree used here holds 82,115 noun concepts.

Now attach one number to each node: how specific is it? Count how many concepts sit underneath it. Entity has everything underneath, so it is the least specific thing there is. Abyssinian cat has almost nothing underneath, so it is very specific.

specificity  =  1  −  log(descendants + 1) ÷ log(total + 1)

The logarithm is there so that going from 10 descendants to 100 counts the same as going from 100 to 1000 — specificity changes by ratio, not by headcount. Nothing here was learned; it is arithmetic on the tree's shape.

So two words have a distance

Find the deepest node both words sit under — their nearest shared ancestor. The distance is how much specificity you throw away climbing to it from each side, added together.

Two of these should stop you.

dog and wolf (0.8331) are farther apart than dog and cat (0.6909) — even though a wolf is obviously more dog-like than a cat is. And democracy and tyranny are the farthest apart of all (1.7217), though they are opposites, and opposites are about the same thing.

Both are correct, because this distance measures shared ancestry, not similarity. Dog and cat both stop at carnivore; wolf is a more specific word, so there is more specificity to throw away getting to the meeting point. Democracy and tyranny share nothing until abstraction, near the very top.

Knowing what a number measures is not optional. This one is exactly as useful as it is — and using it as a similarity score would be wrong in a way nothing would flag.

Every word is a point

Give each word a position, and every pair distance follows from the two positions. Walk from the word up to the root, and at each step write down how much specificity that step cost. Those steps are the coordinates.

A word's distance from the origin is its length — which is what it means to be a real position in a real space. The two agree exactly, to the last digit the machine carries.

And the whole thing is three lines

def sparse_vec(synset, ic):
    """{node c : sqrt(IC(c) - IC(parent(c)))} over the path — the exact JC coordinate."""
    return {name: math.sqrt(d) for name, d in _path_edges(synset, ic) if d > 0.0}

The claim in that comment is strong: the straight-line distance between two of these positions is exactly the tree distance from the previous slide — not an approximation of it.

Checked on 150 randomly chosen pairs of deep words, drawn from the 52,747 concepts that sit at least 8 levels down:

largest disagreement

2.220e-16

what that is

One step of floating-point rounding — the smallest difference this machine can represent at that magnitude. There is no error, only arithmetic.

It works because the steps telescope: walking up from one word and down to the other, every shared step cancels, and what survives is precisely the two climbs to the meeting point.

The identity is specific to the walk. The path taken here follows one parent per word, so there is exactly one climb from each and exactly one meeting point. Where the dictionary gives a word several parents there is no single path upward, and the quantity the identity is about has to be defined before it can be checked.

Squeezing it into a fixed size

There is a problem with those exact positions: every word uses a different set of coordinates, so the space has as many dimensions as there are concepts. The instrument needs a fixed-width frame.

So each coordinate is assigned a slot by hashing its name, and slots are shared. Collisions are real and they cost accuracy — the question is how much.

Monotone, and then flat. Going 2048 → 4096 buys +0.0017 — nothing. The code picks 2048, and 256 for a small device, and those two numbers are where the curve says to put them.

The two widths do not mix: asking for a distance between a 2048-wide position and a 256-wide one raises. A dimension mismatch that passes silently is a wrong answer that looks fine.

Which nouns was that measured on?

Everything above draws its 150 pairs from one place: nouns sitting eight levels or more below the root. That is a reasonable sample and it is not the population either claim is about — neither “the coordinate reproduces the distance” nor “the projection keeps the order” says anything about depth. So both were run again on two more populations, same 150 pairs each, nothing else changed.

The identity does not care. The largest disagreement between the coordinate's squared distance and the tree distance is 2.2e-16 on every population — machine epsilon, on shallow nouns, deep nouns and all 82,115 of them alike. That is what a structural claim looks like when it is actually structural.

The ordering does care, and the sample was the flattering one. At D = 256 the deep-only sample gives 0.7390; across every noun in the dictionary it is 0.6776. Quoting the first as the quality of the projection would overstate what it does on an arbitrary pair of words — which is the case a real caller has.

The shape is worth a moment. Shallow nouns are the easiest of the three, and the mixed population is harder than either half, because mixing depths widens the range of distances the projection has to keep in order.

None of which changes the conclusion that enough dimensions preserve the ordering — at D = 4,096 every population is above 0.96. What it changes is that “enough” depends on the population, and a figure quoted without one is a figure about somebody else's words.

What counts as a letter decides the record

Everything above assumes you can get from a word as written to the concept it names. That step looks trivial and is not. The code that does it once defined “a letter” as A–Z or a–z, and treated every other letter as a separator.

Those fragments are real words. The record kept by the code names where they landed: Zürich became rich and was read as rich people. naïve became na and was read as sodium. El Niño became ni and was read as nickel. Across the 40 accented spellings tested here — place names, loanwords and personal names — the old rule broke 40, and 14 of those landed on a concept the dictionary really holds: the wrong one, with no error and no log line.

The fix is not a bigger list of letters. A character the rule does not recognise now stays inside the word, so Bjørn folds to something the dictionary does not hold, matches nothing, and is reported as unplaced.

“A wrong concept is worse than no concept, and nothing downstream can tell the two apart. Absence is a measurement and a mis-grounding is not.”

What decides the rows, the columns and the cells

A grid of numbers arrived in unit A1 ready to read. Something decided what the rows are, what the columns are, and what each cell means — and every read that follows inherits those decisions completely.

  • The geometry is derived, not learned. One dictionary's shape, one logarithm, and a walk to the root. No corpus was fitted and no weights exist to go stale.
  • The coordinates are exact — agreeing with the closed form to 2.220e-16 — and only after they are squeezed into a fixed width does any approximation enter. The sweep says where to stop squeezing.
  • The hard part was not the mathematics. It was deciding what counts as a letter. The geometry was always exact while Zürich was being read as rich people.

That last one is the general lesson of the whole tier. The instrument is only ever as good as what was handed to it, and the handing-over is where the quiet mistakes live — because a mis-grounded word is still a perfectly valid number.

What these numbers are scoped to: the specificity table is derived from the dictionary's shape, so the figures are correct for that basis and differ from the corpus-derived one the running system uses. A small adapter supplies the interface the geometry expects, so crystal's own code produced every figure above.
