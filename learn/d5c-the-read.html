The Read

Two units fixed the inequality: the floor κ0 from counting, and the tension μ(β) from bounds. This unit is the instrument that takes a lattice field and returns numbers.

The symbols here, said aloud: Δ is delta, the gap; μ is mu, a multiplier per step; ρ is rho; φ is phi; and Σ is a capital sigma, meaning add up what follows. ± is plus or minus and ∞ is infinity.

The gauge configuration is a four-dimensional field of matrices. What the read takes from it is a screen: an ordered axis and a set of channels. The reduction is one line of physics — the local gauge-invariant action density at each site:

φ(x)  =  Σp (1 − &frac1N; Re tr Up)

That is a real number per site, built from the plaquettes Up touching it, and it is gauge-invariant — the same number whatever gauge the configuration is written in. Two reads then run on it, and they answer different questions.

What a rate is

The next slide reports six rates, so it is worth fixing what one is. In this argument a rate means exactly one thing:

a rate of r  =  multiply by e−r every step

A big rate is a fast fall. Translate the six the next slide plants, and walk each one three steps:

The third column is the second one cubed, worked from the four places printed beside it — so what you type is what the table shows.

And back the other way, which is what the instrument does: a factor of 0.7408 per step is a rate of −ln(0.7408) = 0.30. The two forms carry the same fact, and the logarithm is only there because factors multiply and rates add.

You have met this curve three times already. Unit A3 measured it along an ordered axis and called it a point-spread. Unit B3 measured it across a distance and called it survival, e−d/ξ. Unit C4 used it to decide what a running system still treats as current. Here it is on the time axis of a lattice, and the rate has a name: the mass gap.

The instrument, on a field whose answer is known

The decay read identifies a linear propagator from the trajectory and takes the magnitude of its slowest eigenvalue. A mode that is multiplied by μ each step decays at rate −log|μ|, so the slowest mode's rate is the gap:

Δ  =  −log |μ1|

Before pointing that at physics, point it at something with a known answer. A field is built here from 6 modes decaying at rates chosen in advance, and handed to the read:

Every rate returns, to the digits shown. The slowest of them is what the gap read reports: 0.300000 against a planted 0.30.

The read is an eigenvalue of the record's own dynamics. It works out the operator that carries one step into the next, and then asks what that operation multiplies by — those multipliers are its eigenvalues, one per mode, and each is a factor per step. The record supplies them, and no setting decides how many to keep.

Why the read subtracts the vacuum

An equilibrium field sits at a vacuum expectation and fluctuates about it. That constant is itself a mode of the dynamics, and it is the slowest one there is — a constant never decays.

So the slowest mode of the raw dynamics is the vacuum, and a read that takes it reports gapless on every equilibrium ensemble it is ever handed. Measured here on stationary fields whose fluctuations decay at a rate known in closed form:

The raw column is the vacuum, every time. The connected read subtracts the mean first, so the slowest mode it can find is the slowest fluctuation about the vacuum, which is what the gap is.

Δ  =  −log |μ1|   on the connected dynamics

The read comes in low: 0.2777 against a planted 0.3011 at the fastest rate on the table. A slow mode needs a long record to resolve, so a finite record understates the rate — and understating the decay rate understates the gap, which is the conservative direction for a claim that the gap is positive.

The second read: is there long-range order?

The gap read answers how fast, along the time axis. The confinement read answers which phase, and it works on the spatial correlation.

Each L×L spatial plane is kept intact and read on its own, and the results are averaged over planes. Ksignal counts the modes standing above a confined-vacuum reference null — a floor set by a deeply confined ensemble, so the comparison is against the theory's own disorder.

The direction takes a moment, and it is the point of the slide. Ksignal counts coherent modes, so it reads order. Confinement is the disordered phase: the field is scrambled, its spectrum sits in the random-matrix bulk, and nothing resolves. So Ksignal is low exactly where the gap lives, and rises when a massless mode appears and resolves above the sea.

The no-bump

The same read across a coupling range separates the two theories, and the separation is the measurement the argument wants.

groupcouplings read Ksignal rangewhat it says
SU(2)140.0363 – 0.0881flat and low at every coupling
compact U(1)150.0319 – 0.3390steps up past its deconfinement transition

SU(2) stays confined across the whole range, with no rise anywhere — that flatness is the no-bump, and it is the disorder-operator form of the statement the gap argument needs. Compact U(1) has a transition, and the same read finds it: that is what makes the flat SU(2) line evidence about SU(2).

What the instrument contributes

  • One reduction, and it is physics. The action density φ(x) = Σp(1 − &frac1N; Re tr Up) turns a field of matrices into a gauge-invariant screen. Spatial planes stay intact, because the confinement read lives on their correlation.
  • The gap read is an eigenvalue. Δ = −log |μ1| off a propagator identified from the trajectory. Planted rates come back to the digits shown.
  • Subtract the vacuum, or measure it. The slowest mode of an equilibrium field is the vacuum, at rate zero. The connected read is what makes the answer a statement about fluctuations.
  • The read is conservative. A finite record understates a slow rate, so Δ comes in low — the safe direction for a claim that Δ is positive.
  • Ksignal is low where the gap lives. It counts order, and confinement is disorder. The flat SU(2) line beside the U(1) step is the pair that makes each of them readable.
