A Count and a Weight

Unit A14 opened the evidence under a count and showed the count depends on where a line goes. This is the question after it: given the modes that stand up, how much do they carry?

readwhat it answers
Ksignal · resolved_modeshow many modes stand above the noise floor
resolved_powerhow far above it they stand, added up

The first two are the same number. resolved_modes returns exactly what Ksignal returns, on all 10 records in this unit — the terrain, four readings of the EEG, both market records and four draws of noise.

That agreement gets re-run on every build. Three documented relations in this same family fail the same test when measured, which is what makes a surviving one worth stating. A relation that survives is evidence; a relation nobody tests is a sentence.

What the two reads separate is the subject of the next slide, and it shows up on records this course has been reading since unit A1.

Same count, different weight

The last column is σF — the Greek letter sigma, the leading correlation between a record's columns. It is on the table so all three reads can be compared on one row.

the market's prices and the EEG both report one mode. That one mode carries 4.025171 in the first and 0.375320 in the second — 10.7 times the weight, in the same count.

And the row between them settles it. the EEG read as 8 columns reports 3 modes carrying 2.289728, which is less than the market's prices carries with one. A count of three and a count of one, and the count of one is the heavier record.

how many  ≠  how much

Which is why both reads are published. A reader with only the count knows how many things rose above the floor; a reader with the weight knows how much of the record they account for. Unit A12 asks the same shape of question about the columns — four sensors, and how many are carrying — and this asks it about the modes.

Two values a reader can check the scale against

Four draws of noise, two widths, four independent seeds. Nothing was put in any of them:

recordmodesresolved power σF
noise, seed 3, 800×4001.0255
noise, seed 5, 800×8001.0546
noise, seed 7, 400×4001.0772
noise, seed 9, 400×8001.0897

The power is zero. Not small — 0 to every digit a double carries, on all four. Nothing rose above the floor, so nothing is summed, and the read says so with a value that cannot be mistaken for a measurement.

And σF sits at one. Between 1.0255 and 1.0897 on the four noise draws, against 1.2452 to 12.0127 on the six records with something in them. The leading correlation between uncorrelated columns is 1, so a reader meeting σF for the first time has a known value to read it against.

This is the same property that makes unit A12's count usable: give it k equally loaded columns and it returns k, exactly. A scale that lands on a known number in the case you can check by hand is a scale you can use in the cases you cannot — and unit A7 is where the exact zero is the whole subject.

  • The count and the weight are different questions. the market's prices and the EEG both report one mode, carrying 4.025171 and 0.375320. the EEG read as 8 columns reports 3 modes carrying less than either.
  • Two reads agree, and that was checked. resolved_modes returns what Ksignal returns on all 10 records. Three other documented relations in this family do not survive the same test, so this one is re-checked on every build.
  • Nothing gives exactly nothing. Four independent noise draws return a resolved power of 0 and a σF of about 1.
  • Quote both. A mode count with no weight beside it says how many things cleared a floor and nothing about how much of the record they are.

What the weight measures is how far the resolved modes stand above the noise floor, added up. Whether that is a lot depends on the record and on the question, and this number is one input to that — the same footing unit A13 puts its scatter share on. Unit A16 takes the exact zero above and uses it as a ruler: a null with no spread at all is the strongest one there is.
