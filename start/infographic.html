⑩THE SIGNAL
Signed, propagated, conserved

A question arrives as a signal, signed where it enters. It spreads through the corpus, and wherever it meets something that matches, that part condenses while the rest travels on.

Nothing is lost and nothing is created. Absorbed plus transmitted equals incident at every hop — a leak shows up as arithmetic.

What condenses is an artifact. It is signed where it condensed, lands in the lattice, and carries the provenance of everything that fed it.

It travels under the grant it will be stored under. Reading, sharing and invoking are settled once, at the substrate, and the signal is bound by that wherever it goes.

Two questions about one thing differ because the two signals meet at different places on the screen.

When nothing rises above the noise, nothing comes out. No apology, no placeholder, no guess — the empty answer is a measurement.

⑪THE SUBSTRATE
Encrypted, addressed, governed

Everything is an artifact — content, context, provenance and edges, addressed by its own hash. The store holds, versions and searches every kind alike, whatever is inside it.

The hash is the coordinate. A change to the body is a new version, so agreeing on the index is agreeing on the content.

Content and queries are both ciphertext on the encrypted arm. Ranked search runs over blind tokens — the storage layer performs the search without ever reading it. That arm covers 5.9% of the reference corpus today; the rest is served by a plaintext lexical index, which is what it exists to replace.

Read, share, invoke and revoke are one permission set, resolved by reachability. Confidentiality is a separate key scheme whose recipients that reachability computes, so revoking is one edge edit with nothing re-encrypted.

It improves by merging. Two records nothing can separate become one object carrying both their evidence. Fewer things, each one heavier.

One lattice, zero external database processes — which is why the same machinery runs in a browser tab, on a full corpus, or air-gapped.
